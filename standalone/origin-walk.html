Origin Walk

Bacterial chromosome records conventionally begin at the replication origin, next to dnaA — though not all of them do, and E. coli MG1655 famously starts at thrL instead. Each of these 14 genomes is walked from its own first gene: at every locus a genomic language model is asked which protein family comes next, and its answer is set against the gene the genome actually carries.

What to look at first

Read the top lane. Staphylococcus aureus opens on dnaA, and the model answers dnaN — correct, first try. It misses yaaA, an uncharacterised ORF, then recovers recF exactly, with gyrB as its runner-up at that locus — the gene that genuinely comes next. That is the canonical replication-origin cassette, reproduced from one seed gene.

Then scan right. The teal thins out, the entropy bars above grow, and by locus 20 the model is guessing. Lanes disagree because the 50,000-cluster vocabulary splits orthologues across phyla: the dnaA of S. aureus and of M. tuberculosis are not the same family.

The generative model exists — just not in the public collection

preprint, figure 5

Everything above runs on the only published checkpoint that predicts a next family, and that checkpoint takes protein embeddings as input. The Bacformer preprint describes a different model: one finetuned to generate sequences of protein families, prompted with a property token plus a set of starting families, sampled by temperature. It generates whole genomes, and the authors check that none of them are copies of the training corpus.

That model is the right tool for a generated pangenome graph. Its weights are not among the seven checkpoints in the published collection, so the walk here is teacher-forced instead: a real genome supplies the prefix, and the model supplies the expectation at each locus. Read the misses as a property of this checkpoint on a 30-gene prompt, not as a verdict on what the family-level generator would do.

Two details from the same figure are worth knowing before designing anything here. The property token is appended to the front of the sequence, which is where a habitat or lineage token would go. And the paper samples by temperature — the words top-p and nucleus appear nowhere in it, which matches the degeneracy measured above.

Why nucleus branching degenerates here

The plan was to branch on every family inside the top-p nucleus and draw the resulting graph. That cannot work on this model. At every threshold tried, the median locus admits exactly one family — a single candidate already carries the whole mass, so the sweep produces a chain, not a graph. The mean tells the other half of the story: a minority of loci go nearly flat over hundreds or thousands of families. The output is a spike or a plateau, with no usable middle where nucleus sampling lives.

Distance from the origin

locimean entropytop-1 accuracy

The replication origin is the most syntenically conserved stretch of a bacterial chromosome, and the model behaves accordingly: near dnaA it is both more certain and more often right, and it loses the thread with distance. This decay, not the graph, is the measurement worth keeping.

One confound to hold onto. The “real” family at each locus is itself assigned by Bacformer's masked head, not by an independent ground truth. Part of every miss below could be two heads of the same model disagreeing about which of the 50,000 clusters a protein belongs to, rather than the model failing at genome syntax.
