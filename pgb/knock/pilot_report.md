# In-silico knockout of the coupled regions: pilot

Model `macwiatrak/bacformer-causal-complete-genomes` (causal), fp32 weights and activations, one pass per whole chromosome (batch 1, padded to n + 1 positions per genome), readouts decoded in float64. Generated by `pg_knockout.py --pilot` on 2026-09-26 05:25.

## What was measured

- **co-occurrence** links: genomes carrying both elements at their spots; the upstream element U (dnaA reading order, per genome) is deleted (member genes only, never a backbone family); readout = logit P(D entry family) at D's entry gene. Expected: the knockout LOWERS it.
- **avoidance** links: genomes carrying U and cleanly lacking D (no D family anywhere, none at D's spot); readout = log P(D entry family) - log P(resident family) at the row where D's entry WOULD sit, placed from the carriers' context (1 + the gene of the family that precedes D's entry in >= 60 % of carriers, nearest copy within 40 genes; else the gene of the family that follows D's last gene). Expected: the knockout RAISES it.
- P(family | context) = sum over the full 50,001-way softmax of P(cluster | context) x P(family | cluster); the decoder P(family | cluster) comes from the fp32 baseline top-64 calls of the other lineage half.
- delta = readout(knockout) - readout(baseline). z = (delta - median of matched controls) / (1.4826 x MAD), floored at the genome's numerical floor; z_exp = z x expected sign (> 0 = the expected direction). Matched controls: WHOLE accessory elements of the same genome (every coupled element present at its spot, plus every whole panRGP run at a spot with no coupled region), upstream of D, size x0.5-x2 of U's, distance to D within +-35 % of U's, not overlapping U or D and not belonging to a region linked to U or D; windows widened to x1/3-x3 and +-60 % when fewer than 3 match.
- **specificity**: the same U pass is also read at up to 12 other elements of the same kind at a comparable distance (off-targets). spec = z_exp(D) - mean z_exp(off-targets). A deletion that moves everything downstream is not knowledge of the link.
- **calibration**: every per-link p is calibrated empirically by putting one of the unit's own matched controls in U's place (200 draws), because the matched null has skewed tails.

## Self-checks (asserted in every genome; the run stops on a failure)

- (a) delete nothing: 537 passes, max |delta| = 0.0 (exactly 0 required)
- (b) delete 10 genes right after the last readout row: 537 passes, max |delta| = 0.0; and in every one of the 42118 deletion passes plus 360 insertion passes, all readouts before the first modified gene were bit-identical to the baseline (2053737 readouts checked)
- (c) readout rows land on the intended gene after the shift: 5082956 (row, pass) identities and families asserted
- (e) the row convention, against the model: decoded log P(own family) at the readout row beats the same family read one row later by 3.40 on average per genome (worst genome 2.18, > 0.5 required). An off-by-one in which output row is read would reverse this; the pilot's check (c) could not fail.
- (f) numerical floor: the same baseline at a padded length 64 longer moves a readout by at most 1.11e-05 per genome (worst 4.09e-05). Deltas below their genome's floor are flagged unresolved: 90% of units are above it.
- (d) decoder of the other lineage half: 537/537 genomes; decoder saw the genome's lineage in 0 genomes (0 required; its genes asserted absent too)

## Reading the pilot (notes written after the run)

**What this second pilot changed.** The first pilot's avoidance readout was placed at the start of D's panRGP spot block, where D's element almost never begins in carriers; its null was a contiguous sub-run of a natural element, which is not the same kind of object as the whole element U; its z used the mean and sd of 3-5 skewed control deltas with an absolute floor; and no readout other than D's was scored, so a deletion with a genome-wide effect would have counted as knowledge of every link it belongs to. All four are fixed here. Of the 3435 units, 1872 are avoidance units placed from the carriers' flanking context; 749 (link, genome) pairs had no usable anchor and were dropped rather than read at the wrong row.

**The two runs agree where they should and differ where the fix bites.** For the 1563 co-occurrence units present in both runs the readout row and the deleted genes are identical and the delta reproduces to 1.2e-07 (median 3.1e-10), an end-to-end check of the engine between two independently configured runs. For the 1872 avoidance units in both, 1229 readout rows moved, the baseline P(D entry) at the readout rose from 8.7e-04 to 7.5e-02, and the old and new deltas correlate only 0.70: the first pilot's avoidance numbers were measuring the far tail of the softmax, not the element.

**Resolution.** Repeating a baseline at a padded length 64 longer moves a readout by up to 1.1e-05 in the median genome (4.1e-05 in the worst). 90% of units have a |delta| above their own genome's floor. Links whose deltas mostly sit below it are reported as not testable, not as 'no effect': at fp32 the assay cannot see them.

**Link 35 (yidK, yidJ -> lptG, yghS, glcA) is the one link whose deletion effect survives its own null, and it is not specific to D.** Deleting U lowers logit P(D entry) in 87% of 346 units over 184 lineages (median -0.00053), more than any comparable whole-element deletion at the same distance (calibrated q = 0.0065, at the resolution of 2000 pseudo-U draws), with the same sign in both lineage halves (+2.21 / +3.55) and in genomes released in 2024 or later whose lineage has no older genome (+2.21, 31 lineages). But the same deletion shifts the OTHER present elements of that genome by +0.00237 against +0.00038 for its own matched controls (p = 6.1e-09), and D's shift relative to those other readouts is -0.00400, no better than a control deletion's (-0.00034); in raw units D moves significantly LESS than the elements around it (q = 0.0065). Removing U changes what the model expects of accessory content over a wide downstream region, and D travels with it. The z-based specificity test does pass (q = 0.0065), but a z rewards a readout whose own null is tight, which D's is, so it cannot carry the claim alone.

**Links whose effect runs the other way: 0, 7.** 2 of them fail the same specificity test in that direction, so they are the mirror image of the case above: deleting U raises everything downstream. The first pilot's five 'opposite' verdicts are not reproduced as such.

**The converse intervention for link 35.** In 29 genomes (one per lineage) that carry D and cleanly lack U, inserting U's element at U's own panRGP spot 688 genes upstream of D's entry raises logit P(D entry) in 29/29 of them (median +0.00715), against +0.00412 for a whole foreign natural element with the same protein count at the same point (difference p = 0.0000) and +0.00123 for U's own genes at another backbone point at the same distance. So the model does use this content, at this range, and only where it belongs. The same insertion moves the entries of the OTHER present accessory elements at comparable distances by +0.00924, more than D's (paired difference -0.01766, p = 1.000): the same lack of specificity as the deletion.

**The converse intervention for link 7.** In 39 genomes (one per lineage) that carry D and cleanly lack U, inserting U's element at U's own panRGP spot 1701 genes upstream of D's entry raises logit P(D entry) in 22/39 of them (median +0.00004), against +0.00007 for a whole foreign natural element with the same protein count at the same point (difference p = 0.0063) and -0.00001 for U's own genes at another backbone point at the same distance. At 1701 genes the whole effect is at the noise floor, in every arm. The same insertion moves the entries of the OTHER present accessory elements at comparable distances by +0.00001, more than D's (paired difference +0.00038, p = 0.135): the same lack of specificity as the deletion.

**Calibration.** Putting one of a unit's own matched controls in U's place and rerunning the whole per-link test (2000 draws) shows the nominal test rejecting at p < 0.05 in 4.7% (expected direction) and 3.6% (opposite) of draws at the median link, against a nominal 5 %. Individual links reach 65% / 15%. Every q in the table is BH over the calibrated p.

**Global test.** 12 testable links, median link effect +0.06 z units, 8/12 in the expected direction (Wilcoxon p = 0.19); on a region-disjoint subset 8 links, median +0.10 (p = 0.23). The effect does not track the within-lineage coupling strength (Spearman vs |link z| -0.15, vs pairs +0.19). Nor do the off-target effects track each region's plain across-genome correlation with U (Spearman -0.06, p 0.36), so a pure strain-marker reading of the off-target pattern is not supported either.

**Positive control.** A copy of D's whole element span inserted ~1600 genes upstream of D's entry changes that readout by |0.0043|, against |0.0040| for a foreign element of the same size at the same point; at the link's own U-D distance (1604 genes) the two are |0.0044| and |0.0042|. Adding 30 genes of D's real upstream context gives |0.0076|. So at these distances the model reacts to the fact of an insertion, not to what was inserted. That is the frame for every 'none' verdict.

**How far the model looks.** Deleting 2 genes 1 gene before D's entry moves the readout by 6.29, so the readout does register large changes. The same deletion 3 genes away moves it 0.109, 10 genes 0.044, 100 genes 0.0142, 1,000 genes 0.0063, 1,600 genes 0.0038. The links join elements a median 1414 genes apart.

**Deviations from the design, and what is still open**
- The design's control panel (up to 40 whole natural accessory elements at spots holding no coupled region, sized to the U deletions) is impossible as written: a genome has only ~24 such elements and none has fewer than 3-4 member genes, while half the U deletions are 1-2 genes. The first pilot used sub-runs; this run uses whole elements of both kinds, with the coupled ones (median 1 member gene) supplying the small sizes. Matched controls per unit: median 12, at least 3 for 87% of units (24% needed the widened window).
- Avoidance units still require D to be cleanly absent (345 pairs excluded) and the absent element to be downstream (807 pairs would need an insertion and are not tested).
- 2.9% of units are partial knockouts (a copy of one of U's families survives elsewhere); they are excluded from the tests and the same tests with them put back are reported per link. 3 links consist only of such units and are 'nt'.
- The decoder is still the two-lineage-halves one of the design. A leave-one-lineage-out decoder would reduce the units whose readout family is unseen from 0.3 % to 0.1 % of the --all set; no link loses all its units either way.
- The numerical floor is a per-genome maximum over readout rows, so it is conservative; a per-row floor would recover some avoidance units.
- The 2024+ column is now restricted to lineages with no older genome, but the cut-off is not tied to the model's training freeze, which is not published with the checkpoint.

## Additional evidence produced during the re-verification (not in either review)

1. **The `spec` test must be read in raw units as well as in z units.** Normalising each readout by its own
   matched-control scatter rewards a readout that happens to be stable. For link 35 the U deletion moves D more than
   any control deletion moves D (calibrated p at the resolution of 2,000 draws), and more than it moves the other
   elements *in z units*, but in raw logit units it moves the other elements MORE than it moves D. The pipeline now
   computes both and a "knows" verdict needs both; a link that passes its own null but fails the raw specificity is
   labelled "not specific".
2. **The link-35 effect is a broad shift, quantified.** Deleting U35 shifts the other present accessory elements'
   entries in that genome by ~7x what its own matched control deletions do (p < 1e-5 on lineage means), while D35's
   shift relative to those other readouts is exactly what a control deletion produces. This is the inference review's
   blocker, confirmed with a null that is exchangeable with U and with the reviewer's own off-target design.
3. **The same conclusion with off-targets drawn from ALL accessory elements, not only the coupled ones.** In 60 link-35
   genomes, reading every maximal panRGP run's entry 300-1,300 genes downstream (496 readouts) with the pipeline's own
   robust z: D's lineage-mean z_exp is +2.62 (p = 2e-9) and the other elements' is +2.12 (p = 4.4e-7); paired, D minus the
   others is -0.48 (p = 0.97), and D's within-genome percentile among its own genome's elements is 0.59. So the z-based
   specificity result the pipeline reports for link 35 depends on which off-targets are used, while the raw comparison
   and this broader one agree that D is not singled out.
4. **The converse intervention behaves the same way.** In genomes carrying D but cleanly lacking U, inserting U at its
   own panRGP spot ~690 genes upstream raises logit P(D entry) in 29/29 genomes (+0.0072 median), beating a foreign
   element of the same protein count at the same point (+0.0041, p < 0.001) and U's own genes at another point
   (+0.0074, p < 0.001) -- but it raises the other present elements' entries by MORE (+0.0092). Content-specific,
   long-range, and not about D.
5. **Placement matters for the insertion.** Inserted at an arbitrary backbone point at the same distance instead of at
   U's spot, U's genes do nothing (24 genomes, no effect, no excess over a foreign element). So the model's use of
   upstream accessory content is conditioned on the content sitting at a plausible spot.
6. **Range, not pairing, is what the insertions show.** The same converse test on link 7, whose U-D distance is 1,701
   genes, moves D's entry by +0.00004 and the other elements by +0.00001: nothing at all. At link 35's ~690 genes the
   same kind of insertion moves D by +0.0072 and the other elements by +0.0092. So the model's expectations about
   accessory content have a range of a few hundred genes, and inside that range the response is broad rather than
   pairwise.
7. **The positive control at the link's own distance fails.** A copy of D's element inserted ~700 genes upstream of D
   changes D's own entry by |0.00096| against |0.00167| for a foreign element of the same size at the same point: at
   this range the model does not specifically anticipate the element it has already seen.
8. **The anchor rule itself needs validating per region.** For 37 of the 155 regions on the absent side of an avoidance
   link, no flanking family both is consensus in the carriers and reproduces D's entry when the rule is applied to a
   carrier; units of those regions are now skipped instead of being read at a plausible-looking but wrong row.

## What changed in the library and the batch script
## Units and readouts
- **Absent element placed from the carriers' context** (`Data.anchors`, `Data.ins_row`, `Data.anchor_check`).
  The row where D's entry would sit is `1 +` the nearest copy (within 40 genes of the spot's insertion point) of the
  family that precedes D's entry in >= 60 % of carriers; failing that, the position of the family that follows D's last
  gene; failing that the unit is skipped with the reason "no insertion anchor". The rule is validated on the carriers
  themselves (applied to a carrier it must return D's real entry gene) and the validation is written to
  `pilot/anchor_check.parquet`, together with what the model says at D's real entry in carriers.
- **Partial knockouts recorded**: every unit stores `u_fam` (U families present here) and `u_left` (how many survive the
  deletion). Units with `u_left > 0` are excluded from the per-link test, counted in the table, and the test with them
  put back is also reported per link.
- **Non-specific readouts flagged**: `f1_up` counts copies of the readout family upstream of the readout row.
- **Skip reasons written** per (link, genome) to `pilot/skipped.parquet`.

## The null
- **Whole accessory elements instead of sub-runs** (`Data.control_pool`): every coupled element present at its spot in
  that genome (region id kept, so controls linked to U or D are excluded per unit) plus every whole panRGP run at a spot
  with no coupled region, capped at 72 per genome and chosen to cover the U sizes of that genome's units. Verified on the
  stored passes: 100 % of control deletions are exactly one element's member genes, none touches a backbone family. This is the same kind of object as U and has the same size distribution
  (77 % are 1-2 member genes), where the pilot's sub-runs were not exchangeable and matched only 4 controls per unit.
- **Adaptive widening** (`match_controls`): x0.5-2 in size and +-35 % in distance, widened to x1/3-3 and +-60 % when
  fewer than 3 match. Matched controls per unit go from a median of 4 (31 % of units unusable) to a median of 10-13
  (~10 % unusable).
- **Robust centring** (`robust_z`): median and 1.4826 x MAD of the control deltas instead of mean and sd of 3-5 skewed
  values, floored at the genome's own numerical floor instead of an absolute 1e-3.
- **Empirical calibration**: for every unit each of its matched controls is also scored as a pseudo-U (leave-one-out
  against its own matched controls), and the whole per-link test is recomputed over 200 draws of one pseudo-U per unit.
  The reported p is the calibrated one, and the per-link false-positive rate of the nominal test is reported. 2,000 draws,
  so a calibrated p can reach 5e-4 and survive BH over the tested links; the loop is vectorised over draws.

## Specificity (the inference blocker)
- Every U pass is now also read at up to 12 **off-target** readouts: the entry genes of other present coupled elements
  (co-occurrence) or the insertion points of other absent ones (avoidance), within +-35 % of D's distance from U,
  excluding regions linked to U or D. Each is scored with its own matched null.
- `spec = z_exp(D) - mean z_exp(off-targets)`, tested on lineage means and calibrated the same way, AND the same
  comparison in raw readout units (`spec_raw`), because a z rewards a readout whose own null is tight. A **"knows"
  verdict now requires both**; a link that beats its own null but not the off-targets is labelled **"not specific"**, and
  the same requirement applies symmetrically to "opposite".
- A per-link **global-shift** table: the mean shift over the OTHER readouts of the same pass, for the U deletion and for
  its own matched controls, with a one-sided test. This is what distinguishes a link-specific effect from a deletion
  that moves every element downstream. Each off-target also carries `phi_U`, the plain across-genome correlation of its region with U's,
  so a strain-marker effect can be seen directly (`pilot_offtarget.parquet`).

## Numerics
- **Per-genome numerical floor**: one extra pass per genome repeats the baseline at a padded length 64 longer; the
  largest readout change is the floor. Deltas below it are flagged `resolved = False`, and a link whose deltas are
  mostly unresolved is "nt", not "none".
- **A row-convention check that can fail** (self-check (e)): decoded log P(the family that is at the row) must beat the
  same family read one row later, on the mean over the genome's readout rows. The pilot's check (c) was an identity.

## Positive control
- Inserts D's member genes, D's whole element span, and that span preceded by 30 genes of its real upstream context,
  each ~1,600 genes upstream and **at the link's own U-D distance**, against a whole foreign natural element with the
  same protein count at the same point.

## Verdicts and statistics
- "knows" = calibrated q < 0.05 AND specificity q < 0.05 AND sign test p < 0.05 AND same sign in both lineage halves
  AND the deltas resolved; "opposite" = calibrated q < 0.05 the other way AND same sign in both halves;
  "nt" now distinguishes no testable genome / no readout / only partial knockouts / no unit with 3 controls /
  too few lineages / deltas below the numerical floor.
- MDE uses a multiplicity-adjusted alpha; the global test is also run on a region-disjoint subset of links; the 2024+
  column is restricted to genomes whose lineage contains no older genome.
- The per-genome regression arm was dropped: its purpose was to rescue the units the old null could not score, and the
  new null scores them directly.
