Reading the fork

The spine holds for four genes. dnaN, recF, gyrB, yidB are core in all eleven strains, which is the replication cassette every bacterial chromosome carries next to its origin. The model names the first three of them in every single strain — eleven out of eleven at each step, with almost no probability left over for anything else. Then the graph opens.

Its certainty does not survive the opening. Across the first twelve loci the per-step accuracy runs 100, 100, 100, 0, 91, 9, 0, 0, 64, 64, 9, 18 percent. The drop is not a decay, it is a cliff, and it lands exactly where the strains stop agreeing.

The first fork is the dgo operon for galactonate catabolism: most strains carry it, O157:H7 Sakai does not and goes straight to cbrA. Further right the lanes reach the waa locus, which builds the lipopolysaccharide core and is among the most variable stretches of the species. By then almost every node is shell or cloud.

Two strains have no DnaA protein. RefSeq annotates dnaA as a pseudogene in CFT073 and Nissle 1917, so those lanes begin at dnaN and are shifted one locus to keep them aligned.

Every prefix handed to the model is the real protein embeddings of a real chromosome, so this measures the model, not a substitution. The anchor is what makes the difference. Along the replication cassette its first guess is right more than half the time, out of fifty thousand families, and its distribution is nearly a point mass. Past the fork the same model falls to about one guess in six. It has not learned a generic next-gene rule; it has learned the stretches of the chromosome that are actually constrained, and it knows where they stop. The dashed edges are what the rest of the error looks like laid on the graph.

Families are assigned by nearest real protein in embedding space, not by the model's masked head. Across these strains the masked head puts orthologues in the same family in none of the cases tested, because it reads genomic context as well as sequence. Nearest-exemplar agrees across all eleven strains for 90% of genes, and puts dnaA, dnaN, recF and gyrB each in one family.
